Networks stack lines; back-propagation moves them

One neuron draws one line. A hidden layer moves the points so that the next line can split them. The chain rule, run backward, tells each weight which way to move.

M. Soleymani · Sharif University of Technology · MLP.pdf (104 slides) · MLP & Back-prop.pdf (100 slides)

Deck 1 · slides 19–21

One neuron draws one line, and XOR needs two

A perceptron computes step(w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + b). The points where the sum is zero form a line. The neuron says 1 on one side and 0 on the other. Turn the line and slide it. AND and OR reach 0 wrong. XOR stops at 1 wrong.

Deck 1 · slides 44–67, 86–87

Lines combined with AND fence any convex zone; zones combined with OR fence any shape

The slides then remove the third layer. Put N lines around a circle and add their votes. Inside, all N vote yes. Far away, about half of them vote yes. The sum becomes a cylinder: N inside, about N/2 outside. Subtract N/2 and add many cylinders, and one hidden layer fences any shape.

Deck 1 · slides 38–41, 77–80

One hidden layer can do it all, but depth costs far fewer neurons

Take parity: output 1 when an odd number of D inputs are on. One hidden layer needs one detector per odd pattern, 2^(D−1) of them. A chain of XOR blocks needs 3 neurons per block, 3(D−1) in total.

Deck 2 · slides 6–19

A step neuron gives a flat loss, so gradient descent has nothing to follow

Training means: change each weight a little in the direction that lowers the loss. That needs a slope. With step, the count of wrong answers is a staircase in every weight. With σ and cross-entropy, the loss is smooth, and its slope points to the minimum.

Cross-entropy with a sigmoid output gives a short gradient: ∂L/∂z = o − y. Softmax with cross-entropy gives the same form, one entry per class. Move z and see the push.

Deck 2 · slides 28–63

Back-propagation runs the chain rule from the output to every input, one gate at a time

Write the neuron as a graph of small gates. The forward pass stores each value. The backward pass starts with 1 at the output. Each gate multiplies the gradient from above, the upstream gradient, by its own local derivative, and passes the result down.

Deck 2 · slides 65–97

For a whole layer, the weight gradient is δ times the input, and it has the shape of W

A layer computes z = W a + b. Call δ = ∂L/∂z the error of the layer. Each weight Wᵢⱼ connects input j to neuron i, so its gradient is δᵢ · aⱼ. All of them together are one outer product. Switch off neuron 2 with ReLU and see its row.

Four equations carry the whole backward pass. Check every one with the shape rule: a gradient has the same shape as the thing it is the gradient of.

What to remember

Five facts carry both decks

  • One neuron is one line. XOR needs a hidden layer, because no single line separates it.
  • One hidden layer is enough in principle: AND of lines makes zones, cylinders added together make any shape, and pulses make any curve.
  • Depth saves neurons: parity of D inputs needs 2^(D−1) wide neurons but only 3(D−1) deep ones.
  • Learning needs a slope: replace step with sigmoid or ReLU, and use cross-entropy, whose output gradient is o − y.
  • Back-propagation is local × upstream at every gate. For a layer: ∂L/∂W = δ aᵀ, and the gradient has the shape of W.
